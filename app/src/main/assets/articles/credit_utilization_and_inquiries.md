# Credit Utilization Ratio & Hard Inquiries Explained

Even borrowers who have never missed a single payment are sometimes surprised to see their credit score dip by 20–40 points. Two subtle mechanics are usually responsible: **statement-date utilization** and **hard inquiries**.

## Understanding Credit Utilization Ratio (CUR)

`CUR = (Total Credit Card Balances Reported / Total Credit Card Limits) × 100`

Banks typically report your balance to credit bureaus on your **monthly statement generation date**—not on the payment due date.

- **Example:** If your limit is ₹1,00,000 and you spend ₹65,000 on travel, your statement generates at **65% utilization**. Even if you pay the full ₹65,000 before the due date, the bureau records 65% utilization for that cycle.
- **Smart Fix:** Pay down a large spike **2–3 days before your statement generation date** so the reported balance stays under 30%.

## Soft Inquiry vs Hard Inquiry

| Inquiry Type | Triggered By | Impact on Credit Score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Soft Inquiry** | Checking your own score, pre-approved offers | **Zero impact** |
| **Hard Inquiry** | Formal loan or credit card application | Small dip (~5–10 pts) for 3–6 months |

## Avoid "Loan Aggregator Spraying"

- Submitting your PAN to multiple online marketplaces simultaneously can trigger **5–8 hard inquiries** in one week, causing automated rejections at prime banks.
- Shortlist **1 or 2 lenders** using LoanJB's Eligibility and Loan Comparison calculators *before* submitting a formal application.

> Accepting periodic **credit limit enhancements** (without increasing spending) naturally lowers your utilization ratio and strengthens your score.
