# Direct vs Regular Plans & Why Expense Ratios Matter

Every Indian mutual fund scheme regulated by SEBI comes in two variants—**Direct Plan** and **Regular Plan**—with the **exact same fund manager and underlying portfolio**, yet permanently different Net Asset Values (NAVs).

## What Is the Difference?

- **Regular Plan:** Bought through a bank relationship manager, broker, or distributor. The fund house pays an ongoing **trail commission (typically 0.50%–1.10% per year)** out of your fund's assets every single year you stay invested.
- **Direct Plan:** Bought directly from the AMC website, MFCentral, or a direct platform with **zero distributor commission**—resulting in a lower **Total Expense Ratio (TER)** and a higher NAV every day.

## How a 0.80% Expense Gap Compounds Over 20 Years

Assume **₹15,000/month SIP for 20 years** in two versions of the same fund:

| Plan Type | Net Assumed Return | Indicative 20-Yr Corpus | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct Plan** (TER 0.50%) | 12.0% p.a. | ~₹1.49 Crore | Baseline |
| **Regular Plan** (TER 1.30%) | 11.2% p.a. | ~₹1.35 Crore | **~₹14.0 Lakh less** |

## When Regular Plans Still Make Sense

- If you genuinely need ongoing hand-holding, behavioural coaching during market crashes, and portfolio servicing from a trustworthy distributor, the trail fee is the cost of that service.
- If you select and track your own funds—or work with a **SEBI-Registered Investment Adviser (RIA)** on a flat fee—**Direct Plans** avoid compounding percentage-based drag.

> Check the scheme name on your CAS statement: it explicitly contains the word **`Direct`** or **`Regular`**. Switching from Regular to Direct triggers exit load (if within exit window) and capital gains tax, so plan switches carefully.
