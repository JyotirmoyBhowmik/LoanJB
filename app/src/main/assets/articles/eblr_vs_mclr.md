# EBLR vs MCLR: Why RBI Repo Rate Cuts Reach You Faster

When the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) changes the **Policy Repo Rate**, floating-rate home and MSME loan borrowers often wonder why their EMIs adjust at very different speeds. The answer lies in the **interest rate benchmark** written into your loan agreement.

## What Is EBLR?

Since October 2019, RBI mandated that banks link all new floating-rate retail loans (home, auto, personal) to an **External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR)**—most commonly the RBI Repo Rate.

- **Formula:** `Effective Rate = Repo Rate + Bank Spread + Credit Risk Premium`
- **Reset Frequency:** Banks must reset EBLR loans **at least once every 3 months**.
- **Transmission:** When RBI cuts the repo rate by 25 bps, your bank must pass it through on your next quarterly reset date.

## How Does Legacy MCLR Differ?

Older loans (sanctioned between April 2016 and September 2019) were linked to **MCLR (Marginal Cost of Funds-based Lending Rate)**, an internal bank benchmark.

| Feature | EBLR (Repo-Linked) | MCLR (Internal) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benchmark | RBI Repo Rate / T-Bill | Bank's internal funding cost |
| Typical Reset | Every 3 months | Every 6 or 12 months |
| Rate Cut Speed | Fast and transparent | Slower, lags RBI moves |
| Rate Hike Impact | Passes through faster too | Delayed reset |

## Should You Switch From MCLR to EBLR?

> Check your latest loan statement. If your benchmark says **MCLR**, **Base Rate**, or **BPLR**, compare your current rate against your bank's prevailing EBLR home loan rate.

1. Request a **rate conversion quote** from your existing bank (often a small flat conversion fee of 0.25%–0.50% + GST).
2. Calculate the break-even months using the Balance Transfer / EMI calculator.
3. Under RBI guidelines, banks must also offer a clear choice between **EMI increase vs tenure extension** whenever floating rates rise.
