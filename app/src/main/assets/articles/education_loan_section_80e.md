# Education Loan Interest Deduction Under Section 80E

Unlike home loans, an education loan enjoys an **uncapped interest tax deduction** under **Section 80E** of the Income Tax Act (available under the **Old Tax Regime**).

## Key Rules of Section 80E

- **No Upper Rupee Limit:** 100% of the **interest component** actually paid during the financial year is deductible—whether it is ₹50,000 or ₹4,00,000.
- **8-Year Window:** Available for a maximum of **8 consecutive assessment years**, starting from the year you first begin repaying interest—or until interest is paid in full, whichever is earlier.
- **Principal Is Not Deductible:** Only interest qualifies under Section 80E (principal repayment does *not* qualify under 80C).
- **Eligible Institutions:** Loan must be taken from an Indian bank, approved financial institution, or notified charitable institution—loans from friends, relatives, or unapproved foreign lenders do **not** qualify.
- **Eligible Beneficiaries:** Higher education (after Class 12, in India or abroad, full-time or vocational) for **self, spouse, children**, or a student for whom you are a legal guardian.

## Strategic Tip: Pay Simple Interest During Moratorium

1. Many students skip paying interest during college years. That unpaid interest gets added to principal, and you earn **zero tax benefit** while in college if you have no income.
2. However, if a **parent in the 30% tax bracket** (Old Regime) is the co-borrower paying simple interest during the course, they can immediately claim Section 80E deduction and shrink the effective interest rate by 30%!

> Remember: Section 80E is **not allowed in the New Tax Regime**. Compare total tax payable across both regimes before choosing.
