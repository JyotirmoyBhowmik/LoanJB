# FD vs RD vs Debt Funds: Post-Tax Return Comparison

Fixed Deposits (FDs), Recurring Deposits (RDs), and Debt Mutual Funds all serve safe-money goals—yet their **compounding mechanics, liquidity, and tax timing** produce noticeably different post-tax wealth.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Dimension | Fixed Deposit (FD) | Recurring Deposit (RD) | Debt Mutual Fund |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Investment Style | Lump sum | Monthly instalment | Lump sum or SIP |
| Compounding | Typically quarterly | Typically quarterly | Daily NAV growth |
| Tax Timing | **Every year** on accrual | **Every year** on accrual | **Only on redemption** (tax deferred) |
| TDS Applicability | Yes, above annual bank cap | Yes, above annual bank cap | No TDS for resident Indians |
| Premature Exit | 0.5%–1.0% rate penalty | 0.5%–1.0% rate penalty | Usually ₹0 exit load (liquid/short-term) |

## Why Tax Deferral Matters for 30% Slab Investors

- On a **7.25% cumulative FD**, an investor in the **30% slab (+4% cess = 31.2%)** pays tax *every year* out of pocket on accrued interest—pulling the real post-tax yield down to **~4.99% p.a.**
- On a **Debt Mutual Fund** earning the same 7.25% gross return, gains are still taxed at slab rate *when redeemed*—but **not paying tax each year** lets the full pre-tax gain compound uninterrupted until withdrawal!
- Meanwhile, **PPF (EEE tax-free)** at 7.10% is equivalent to a **~10.3% pre-tax FD** for someone in the 30% tax bracket.
