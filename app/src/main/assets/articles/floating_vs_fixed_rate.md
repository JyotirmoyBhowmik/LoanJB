# Floating vs Fixed Interest Rates: Which Suits Your Loan?

Choosing between a **floating rate** and a **fixed rate** is one of the biggest structural decisions on a multi-year loan. Both come with distinct cost and prepayment trade-offs in India.

## How Floating Rates Work

- Linked to an external benchmark (EBLR / Repo Rate) plus a fixed spread.
- **Zero prepayment or foreclosure penalty** for individual borrowers on floating-rate term loans per RBI rules.
- Usually starts **1.0% to 2.5% lower** than comparable fixed-rate loans.
- Your EMI or tenure fluctuates when RBI changes the repo rate.

## How Fixed & Hybrid Rates Work

- Interest rate stays locked for the entire tenure (or an initial 2–5 years for **hybrid / teaser** loans, after which it converts to floating).
- Fixed-rate loans often carry a **2%–4% foreclosure penalty** if repaid early from own funds or refinanced.
- Read the fine print for a **"force majeure / reset clause"**—some fixed loans allow the bank to revise rates after 3–5 years anyway.

| Loan Type | Usually Better Choice | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home Loan (15–25 yrs) | **Floating (EBLR)** | Lower starting rate + zero prepayment penalty |
| Car Loan (3–7 yrs) | Fixed (if low spread) | Predictable short-term EMI; check foreclosure clause |
| Personal Loan (2–5 yrs) | Fixed (standard) | Most Indian personal loans are fixed-rate |

> For any loan longer than 7 years—especially a home loan—the ability to make **penalty-free part-payments** on a floating EBLR loan usually outweighs the comfort of a higher-cost fixed rate.
