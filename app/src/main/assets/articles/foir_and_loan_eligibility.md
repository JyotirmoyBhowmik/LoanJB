# FOIR Explained: How Banks Decide Your Maximum Loan

Two applicants with the exact same salary can receive vastly different loan approvals. The key underwriting metric Indian lenders use is **FOIR — Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio**.

## What Is FOIR?

FOIR measures the percentage of your **net monthly income** already committed to fixed debt obligations plus the proposed new EMI:

`FOIR % = (Existing EMIs + Proposed New EMI) / Net Monthly Income × 100`

### Typical Indian Bank FOIR Bands

| Net Monthly Income Band | Typical Allowed FOIR | Safe Personal Comfort Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Below ₹50,000 | 40% – 45% | Up to 35% |
| ₹50,000 – ₹1,50,000 | 45% – 50% | Up to 40% |
| Above ₹1,50,000 | 50% – 55% (occasionally 60%) | Up to 45% |

## What Counts as a Fixed Obligation?

- Existing home, car, personal, or education loan EMIs
- Active **credit card EMI conversions** and Buy-Now-Pay-Later (BNPL) instalments
- Minimum due obligations on revolving credit card balances
- Recurring statutory deductions already netted out before take-home salary

## Practical Ways to Boost Eligibility Safely

1. **Close small high-EMI personal/consumer loans** before applying for a home loan—removing a ₹10,000 EMI can unlock ₹11–12 lakh in home loan eligibility.
2. **Add an earning co-applicant** (spouse or parent) to pool eligible monthly income.
3. **Check retirement age caps:** Most salaried home loans must conclude by age 60 (65–70 for self-employed), which caps maximum tenure.

> Just because a bank approves a 55% FOIR does not mean you should stretch that far. Keeping total EMIs under **35%–40% of take-home pay** leaves room for SIPs, emergencies, and interest rate hikes.
