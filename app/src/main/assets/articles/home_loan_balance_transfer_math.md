# Balance Transfer Math: Calculating True Break-Even Month

Switching your existing home or personal loan to another bank for a lower interest rate—a **Balance Transfer (BT)**—can save lakhs, *or* actually lose money if switching fees eat up the savings.

## What Costs Go Into a Balance Transfer?

Before comparing interest rates, total every upfront switching charge:

- **New Lender Processing Fee + 18% GST**
- **Legal & Technical Valuation Charges**
- **MODT / Memorandum of Deposit of Title Deed** stamp duty on the fresh mortgage (0.1%–0.5% in many states)
- **Foreclosure Penalty** (0% for individual floating-rate home loans, but often 2%–4% + GST on fixed-rate or personal loans)

## How Break-Even Month Works

`Break-Even Months ≈ Total Switching Cost / Monthly Interest Savings`

If switching costs ₹35,000 and saves ₹2,500 per month in interest, you need **14 months** just to recover your switching fees. Real net savings begin only from Month 15 onward.

## Three Rules of Thumb for Indian Borrowers

1. **Negotiate with your existing bank first:** Ask for a **rate conversion** to their current EBLR rate. A ₹5,000 internal conversion fee avoids fresh MODT, legal checks, and document transfer hassle.
2. **Switch early in the tenure:** A 0.50% rate drop in Year 3 of a 20-year loan yields massive savings; the same 0.50% drop in Year 17 barely moves the needle.
3. **Avoid resetting your tenure back to 20 years:** If you have 13 years left, insist on a **13-year tenure** with the new lender—otherwise lower EMIs disguise higher total interest.
