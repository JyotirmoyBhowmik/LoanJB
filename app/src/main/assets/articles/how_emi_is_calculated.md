# How EMI Is Calculated: Reducing Balance vs Flat Rate

An **Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI)** splits every monthly payment into two parts: **interest** on the remaining principal and **principal repayment**. Understanding the underlying math protects you from misleading "low flat rate" offers.

## The Standard Reducing-Balance Formula

Indian banks calculate home, personal, and car loans on a **monthly reducing balance** basis:

`EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)`

- `P` = Loan principal amount
- `r` = Monthly interest rate (`Annual Rate / 12 / 100`)
- `n` = Total number of monthly instalments

In the early years of a long-tenure loan, interest makes up **70%–85%** of each EMI because the outstanding principal is highest. As principal falls each month, the interest share shrinks and principal repayment accelerates.

## Why "Flat Rate" Loans Cost Nearly Double

Some dealer-financed vehicle loans, consumer durable loans, and informal lenders quote a **flat interest rate**, where interest is charged on the *original* principal for the entire tenure—even after you have repaid half the loan.

| Quoted Rate (3 Years, ₹5 Lakh) | Monthly EMI | Total Interest | Effective Reducing Rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10.0% Reducing Balance | ₹16,134 | ₹80,809 | 10.00% p.a. |
| 10.0% Flat Rate | ₹18,056 | ₹1,50,000 | ~17.92% p.a. |

## Quick Checklist Before Signing

> Always ask for the **Key Fact Statement (KFS)** mandated by RBI. It discloses the **Annual Percentage Rate (APR)**—the true annualized cost including processing fees and insurance.

- Never compare a flat rate directly with a reducing-balance rate.
- Check whether interest starts accruing from the **disbursement date** (broken-period / pro-rata interest).
