# HRA Exemption Rules, Metro vs Non-Metro & Landlord PAN

If you live in a rented house and receive **House Rent Allowance (HRA)** as part of your salary, Section 10(13A) under the **Old Tax Regime** can cut your taxable income significantly.

## The Least-of-Three Exemption Rule

Your exempt HRA is the **lowest** of these three figures (calculated on `Salary = Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance forming part of retirement benefits`):

1. **Actual HRA received** from your employer.
2. **Rent paid minus 10% of Salary** (`Actual Rent - 0.10 × (Basic + DA)`).
3. **50% of Salary** if you live in a **Metro** (Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai) OR **40% of Salary** for **Non-Metro** cities (including Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad).

## Rent Receipts & Compliance Rules

- **Annual Rent > ₹1,00,000:** You must submit your **landlord's PAN** to your employer (or a signed declaration if the landlord genuinely does not hold a PAN).
- **Monthly Rent > ₹50,000:** Under Section 194-IB, tenants must deduct **2% TDS** (per updated Finance Act rules) on rent paid to resident landlords and file Form 26QC.
- **Paying Rent to Parents:** Legally permitted if the property is in your parent's name, rent is transferred via bank/UPI, and your parent declares that rental income in their own ITR.

## Can You Claim Both HRA and Home Loan Benefits?

Yes—under the Old Tax Regime, if you own a home in one city (or another locality for genuine work-commute reasons) and genuinely pay rent where you live, you may claim both HRA and Section 24(b) subject to documentation.

> Keep a signed **rent agreement**, monthly bank transfer trail, and properly dated rent receipts (with revenue stamp if paid in cash above ₹5,000) to avoid AIS/ITR mismatch notices.
