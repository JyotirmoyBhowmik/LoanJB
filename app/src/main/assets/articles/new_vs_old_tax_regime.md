# New vs Old Tax Regime: Finding Your Break-Even Deductions

India's Income Tax system gives individual taxpayers a choice each year (for salaried individuals without business income) between the **New Tax Regime** (default, lower slab rates, higher Section 87A rebate, minimal deductions) and the **Old Tax Regime** (higher slab rates, full deductions).

## Key Structural Differences

| Feature | New Tax Regime (Default) | Old Tax Regime (Optional) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slab Rates | Lower, wider tax slabs | Higher slabs (20% / 30% start earlier) |
| Standard Deduction (Salaried) | Higher standard deduction | ₹50,000 |
| Sec 80C / 80D / HRA / 24(b) SO | **Not allowed** | **Allowed** |
| Employer NPS Sec 80CCD(2) | **Allowed** (up to 14% private/govt rules) | Allowed (up to 10%/14%) |
| Paperwork & Proofs | Minimal | Rent receipts, investment & loan proofs |

## The "Break-Even Deduction" Concept

Because the New Regime charges lower slab rates and offers a generous tax rebate for lower/middle incomes, the Old Regime only wins when your **total eligible deductions** (HRA + 80C + 80D + 24(b) home loan interest + 80CCD(1B)) cross a **break-even threshold** for your income level.

- For many salaried earners with **no HRA and no large home loan**, the **New Tax Regime** yields lower tax *and* avoids locking cash into low-return tax-saving products.
- For earners in metro cities claiming **high HRA + ₹2L Home Loan Interest + ₹1.5L 80C + ₹50k–₹75k 80D + ₹50k NPS**, the **Old Regime** can still win—always run the exact numbers!
