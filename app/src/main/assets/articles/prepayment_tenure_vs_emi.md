# Prepayment Strategy: Reduce Tenure vs Reduce EMI

When you make a lump-sum part-payment on your loan, your bank offers two choices: **keep your EMI the same and reduce the remaining tenure**, or **keep the tenure the same and reduce your monthly EMI**. Mathematically, one saves dramatically more money.

## A Concrete Indian Home Loan Example

Assume a **₹50 Lakh home loan at 8.50% p.a. for 20 years** (EMI ≈ ₹43,391). Suppose you prepay **₹5 Lakh** at the end of Year 3:

| Option Chosen | New Monthly EMI | Remaining Tenure | Total Interest Saved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Reduce Tenure** (same EMI) | ₹43,391 | Drops by ~39 months | **~₹11.9 Lakh** |
| **Reduce EMI** (same tenure) | ₹38,650 | Unchanged (17 yrs) | **~₹4.6 Lakh** |

Reducing tenure saves **more than 2.5× the interest** because your unchanged EMI continues attacking a smaller principal balance every single month.

## When Should You Choose Reduce EMI Instead?

- Your monthly cash flow is genuinely tight (e.g., temporary income dip, medical expense, or new dependent).
- You need to lower your **FOIR** before applying for another essential facility.

## Best Practices for Part-Payments

1. **Prepay early in the loan tenure:** A ₹1 lakh prepayment in Year 2 saves far more interest than ₹1 lakh in Year 15.
2. **Confirm "Reduce Tenure" in writing / netbanking:** Some banks default to EMI reduction unless you explicitly select tenure reduction.
3. **Never drain your emergency fund:** Maintain at least 6 months of living expenses + EMIs in liquid cash before making lump-sum prepayments.

> Every rupee prepaid on an 8.5% home loan earns a **risk-free, tax-free 8.5% post-tax return** (assuming you are in the New Tax Regime with no Section 24(b) deduction).
