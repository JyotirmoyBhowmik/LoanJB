# Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) for Monthly Cash Flow

While an SIP builds wealth during your earning years, a **Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)** converts a lump-sum corpus into a predictable monthly income stream—especially popular for retirees and sabbatical planning.

## How SWP Works

You instruct the mutual fund to redeem a fixed rupee amount (say **₹50,000 on the 5th of every month**) directly into your bank account while the remaining corpus stays invested and keeps compounding.

## Why SWP Can Be More Tax-Efficient Than FD Interest or IDCW

- In a **Bank FD** or mutual fund **IDCW (Dividend)** option, **100% of the payout** is taxed at your income slab rate every year.
- In an **SWP**, each ₹50,000 withdrawal is a mix of **original principal (not taxed)** plus **capital gains (only the gain portion is taxed)**.

### Simple Example

If you invest ₹50 Lakh and withdraw ₹50,000 after 13 months when the fund grew 10%:
- Roughly ₹45,455 is return of your own principal (**₹0 tax**).
- Only **₹4,545** is capital gain—and in an equity-oriented fund, long-term gains enjoy the annual LTCG exemption limit!

## Sequence-of-Returns Risk (Critical Warning)

> Never run an aggressive SWP directly from a 100% small-cap or volatile equity fund. A sharp 30% market drop in Year 1 forces you to redeem too many units early, permanently exhausting the corpus.

1. Keep your **withdrawal rate conservative** (typically **4%–6% p.a.** initially if adjusting for inflation).
2. Use a **bucket strategy**: hold 2–3 years of withdrawals in liquid/short-duration debt funds and replenish from equity/hybrid buckets during strong market years.
