# XIRR vs CAGR & Mutual Fund Capital Gains Tax Rules

Looking at "absolute return" on a multi-year SIP can severely mislead you. To judge real mutual fund performance—and estimate taxes before redeeming—you need **XIRR** and India's **Capital Gains** rules.

## Why CAGR Fails for SIPs (Use XIRR Instead)

- **CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)** works only for a **single lump-sum investment** made on one date and redeemed on another.
- **XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return)** calculates the true annualized return when you have **multiple cash flows on different dates** (monthly SIPs, top-ups, partial withdrawals, or SWPs).
- Always enter outflows (investments) as **negative** numbers and inflows (redemptions / current value) as **positive** numbers in LoanJB's **XIRR Calculator**.

## Indian Mutual Fund Taxation Basics

Tax treatment depends on the fund's **equity allocation** and your **holding period** (using **FIFO — First In, First Out**—meaning each monthly SIP instalment has its own separate 12-month clock!):

| Fund Category | Short-Term Holding (STCG) | Long-Term Holding (LTCG) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Equity Funds** (≥65% domestic equity) | ≤ 12 months → Special STCG rate | > 12 months → Annual exemption + LTCG rate |
| **Specified Debt Funds** (≤35% equity, bought post Apr 2023) | Any period → Taxed at **your slab rate** | No indexation / slab rate |
| **Hybrid / Gold / International** (35%–65% equity or FoFs) | Check holding period (24m rules) | Special LTCG rate without indexation |

> Tax rates and LTCG exemption limits change with Finance Acts. Always verify current thresholds in LoanJB's **MF Tax on Gains** calculator and **Data & Rates** screen before redeeming.
